# Research roadmap

Last updated: 10 August 2026

This roadmap records intended work, not completed findings or promised
publications. Each stage has a gate so that adding projects does not weaken the
quality of the current study.

## Stage 1 — instruction-placement study

Status: in progress.

- Obtain independent protocol and analysis review.
- Freeze exact agent/model configuration, exclusions, repetitions, and budget.
- Execute the protected holdout without altering hypotheses after inspection.
- Release real records, analysis, uncertainty, failures, and deviations.
- Prepare a manuscript or preprint only after reproduction and author review.

Gate: no confirmatory execution until the public review and design-freeze issues
are resolved.

## Stage 2 — evaluation-oracle integrity

Status: planned; three development tasks provide only a feasibility check.

- Complete a fresh primary-source novelty review.
- Select openly licensed benchmark samples independently of Stage 1's holdout.
- Predeclare seeded-baseline, known-safe-control, contract, and mutation checks.
- Separate automated flags from manually confirmed defects.
- Notify maintainers privately before making public defect claims.
- Seek independent reproduction of a documented sample.

Potential output: a reusable audit tool, versioned evidence dataset, repairs, and
a technical report or artifact paper. No defect rate or general adequacy claim
exists yet.

## Stage 3 — coding-agent reproducibility and version drift

Status: planned.

- Select a legally and computationally reproducible published result.
- Freeze a compact public task suite and exact environment records.
- Repeat it across agent/model/toolchain versions or time.
- Distinguish version drift from dependency drift and stochastic variation.
- Report negative replications as carefully as successful ones.

Gate: begin only after Stage 1 has a stable artifact and Stage 2 has a reviewed
protocol.

## External validation

Across the stages, the project seeks substantive upstream contributions,
independent review or subset reproduction, and an externally observed research
presentation. Git history proves changes to this repository; it does not by
itself prove scientific validity, authorship, peer review, or publication.

Status labels are used literally:

- `planned`: scoped but no executed study;
- `in progress`: real work exists but required gates remain;
- `preprint`: a public manuscript not peer reviewed;
- `submitted`: under venue review;
- `accepted`: accepted by the named venue.
